Market Summary

What's changed since this morning — and what it means for today's pipeline

This morning's Middle East‑driven oil shock hasn't cooled off into the afternoon — it's extended. Brent crude, which briefly topped $100/barrel around this morning's open, has pushed further to a fresh session high near $101 as fighting around the Gulf of Oman and Saudi energy infrastructure continues, with oil now up roughly 60% year‑to‑date. Bond markets have kept leaning into the move: the 10‑year Treasury, which was already back to about 4.81% this morning (matching last week's post‑jobs‑report cycle high), has ticked up further to roughly 4.84% this afternoon — a fresh high for the cycle — while the 2‑year is holding steady near 4.43%. U.S. equity indexes opened lower on the day (Dow down roughly 0.75%–1.2%, S&P 500 and Nasdaq both softer), which would normally support bond prices, but Treasury yields rose anyway, underscoring that the oil‑driven inflation story is the dominant factor in today's pricing rather than a simple risk‑off rotation.

MND's own 30‑year and 15‑year rate index, along with the UMBS coupon settle prices, still hadn't posted a new print as of this refresh — both remain at Tuesday's confirmed 6.89%/6.49% and 98‑22/101‑00 levels, with today's official numbers due later this afternoon. Given how the session has continued to move against bonds since the morning, a modest‑to‑firm increase in both looks more likely than not once the print lands. Third‑party trackers stayed mixed: Zillow's marketplace average (via Yahoo) shows the 30‑year up a handful of basis points to 6.73% and the 5/1 ARM breaking back above 7%, while at least one other consumer‑facing source described today's tone as "mixed with upward pressure" even while its own quoted rate table showed a modest decline — a reminder that these aggregators can disagree with each other and even with themselves, and shouldn't be read as a preview of MND's own afternoon print. A separate survey of economists now shows roughly 30% expecting a Fed rate hike at the September 15–16 meeting, up sharply from just 10% in August, broadly consistent with CME FedWatch/Polymarket odds that have held near 60% since Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole remarks.

The data calendar stayed light today: MBA's weekly applications survey for the week ending 9/4, expected around 7am ET, still hadn't surfaced in available sources as of this midday check — last week's release (ending 8/28) showed a modest 0.8% rebound in volume, purchase apps up 2%, and refis down 1%, with ARM selections at a five‑week high. Freddie Mac's PMMS remains at 6.71%, unchanged for a third straight week; the next update lands tomorrow. The bigger story stays what's ahead: August PPI lands Thursday and August CPI on Friday, the last major inflation reads before the Fed's meeting, and today's continued oil move raises the stakes on both. For today's pipeline, the case for flagging reprice risk is, if anything, a little stronger than it was this morning — MBS pricing is already carrying forward a lower Tuesday settle, yields have kept climbing into the afternoon, and the official print hasn't even posted yet. For any borrower closing in the next one to two weeks, or anyone on the fence about locking ahead of Thursday's PPI or Friday's CPI, this remains a reasonable afternoon to check in and walk through the numbers before the close. For closings well outside that window, today's move doesn't change the underlying calculus much, but it's worth watching closely given how much data lands over the next 48 hours.
